# Genre render review, 2026-11-19 (the re-read after the R's leg root changed)

WHAT IT'S FOR: the record behind the dated verdicts in `references/genres.md`, replacing
`docs/reviews/genres-2026-11-16.md` as the one `tests/test_genres.py` holds the recipes to. The 0.5.0 change to the
capital `R` (`docs/plan-r-leg.md`: the leg's start became a T-join, `i`) moved every recipe's outline digest, and the
rule in `genres.md` section 6 is that a stale digest means a new reading and a new record, not an edited digest. This
record says what was read this time, what was concluded per recipe, and the digests of the outlines as drawn now.

## What was reviewed

- The page built by `node scripts/concepts/build-page.js --template round1 --concepts templates/concepts.genres.js --content scripts/concepts/content.genres.json`
  with `scripts/gen.js` carrying the T-join, served with the built neutral Sampler beside it, and shot by
  `scripts/shoot.py` at 1280 px, 2x, `--height 16000 --dump-dom`, in headless Chrome. The DOM reported all ten recipes
  drawn (`data-fd-rendered="3330"`). The screenshots are not kept in the repository.
- Read by one model reviewer (the implementer, not a fresh reviewer: the operator chose the full re-read, and the
  reading is of one letter whose change is measured, not of the genres), as crops of the shot: for every recipe, the
  banner's 80 px "Harbour Review" (its `R` enlarged twice, with and without the stem) and the 22 px spaced-capitals
  wordmark "HARBOUR REVIEW" (both `R`s). The rest of each recipe was read only for what the change could touch:
  nothing else changed, by measurement (`tests/test_r_leg_join.py::test_only_the_r_changed_since_0_4_0`), so the
  2026-11-16 verdicts on contrast, serifs, diagonals and the other letters stand as they were written and were not
  re-judged.
- What was seen, per recipe, at the `R`: the leg leaves the bowl's closing bar in a sharp notch on the bar's lower
  edge, the counter above the bar is clean, and nothing round or square shows at the join, in all ten (didone,
  fatface, scotch, clarendon, slab, grot, geometric, reverse, opsz-text, opsz-display) at 80 px and in the wordmark.
  On 2026-11-16 the serif recipes and the optical pair showed a round lump in the counter at that join (measured on
  0.4.0: 15 to 25 units on these recipes; `reverse`, `grot` and `geometric` had none there, which is why the first
  reading blamed `reverse`'s lump on "the leg": it is at the bar's other end, where the bowl's taper-and-disk meets
  the thin stem, on `P`, `B` and `R`, and it is still there; the crops here did not include the stem, and that lump
  was measured, not re-read: `genres.md` section 7).
- The reading changed no verdict. The `reverse` text no longer blames the `R`'s leg and names the stem-side lump
  instead; the `clarendon` and `slab` texts keep "Rendering defects: section 7", which for them means the butt-ended
  curves of issue #1 item 1.

## Verdicts

| recipe | verdict | date | what was seen |
|---|---|---|---|
| didone | partly | 2026-11-19 | Moderate contrast because of the hairline floor, and equal-weight diagonals on A V W M N v w x y, so it reads as a light modern, not a Didone. The diagonals need an expansion model, which is out of scope |
| fatface | no | 2026-11-19 | Stems are about 1/6 of the cap height even at the Bold; W past its range cannot reach 1/3-cap stems without collapsing the counters. It needs new shapes |
| scotch | reaches | 2026-11-19 | A near-twin of the Didone at these sizes; the bracket and the a terminal are what differ. Also shipped and reviewed as Bract's foil on hopsafari.com (2026-11-15). The italic is refused (section 4) |
| clarendon | no | 2026-11-19 | The serifs are short pointed wedges, not square bracketed slabs, so it reads as a wedge serif. A longer serifLen with serifGrow 0 might fix it; untested. Rendering defects: section 7 |
| slab | reaches | 2026-11-19 | Square unbracketed slabs at stem weight, low contrast, in Rockwell or Memphis proportion. Rendering defects: section 7 |
| grot | partly | 2026-11-19 | A monoline neo-grotesque with level-cut joints and a straight-leg R; the double-storey g with its sharp ear and the hooked a read humanist, which needs skeleton work. Seen after the cut default was fixed (section 7) |
| geometric | no | 2026-11-19 | A wide humanist sans, not Futura-like: it needs a circular O, a single-storey a and pointed apexes, all unbuilt |
| reverse | partly | 2026-11-19 | Reverse contrast, but light, at typewriter weight; a larger W and a lower thin might reach a French Clarendon (untested). Rendering defects, and a lump where the bowls of P, B and R meet the thin stem: section 7 |
| opsz-text | reaches | 2026-11-19 | As a pair: a wider, looser text cut with a taller x-height beside a narrower, higher-contrast display cut. The display cut is close to the Didone |
| opsz-display | reaches | 2026-11-19 | As a pair: a wider, looser text cut with a taller x-height beside a narrower, higher-contrast display cut. The display cut is close to the Didone |

## Outline digests

For each recipe: sha256 over `[mult, key, polygons]` for every key of `G` in sorted order, roman, mult 1, 1.25 then 1.5, each coordinate
rounded to 0.01, as `tests/test_genres.py::current_digests()` computes it, from `scripts/gen.js` as it stands with the T-join. Recorded
under node major 26 (this machine). CI runs 20 and 22: those majors are not recorded here, because the machine that wrote this record could
not run them (`npx -y node@<major>` was refused), so on them the test fails with the instructions to record the major; record each from the CI
log's `current_digests()` output and add it under `by_node_major` (on 2026-11-16 the three majors came out identical). The test recomputes this under the running node and fails with "the genre verdict for <id> is stale" when the digest recorded
for that node major differs, and with instructions to record it when the major is not listed. If it fails after a deliberate change to a
recipe or to `gen.js`, the remedy is a new render review (`genres.md` section 6) and a new record, not editing the digest.
