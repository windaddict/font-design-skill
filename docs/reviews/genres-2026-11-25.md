# Genre render review, 2026-11-25 (the re-read after a `cut` end began to pin the samples its corner moved past)

WHAT IT'S FOR: the record behind the dated verdicts in `references/genres.md`, replacing
`docs/reviews/genres-2026-11-22.md` as the one `tests/test_genres.py` holds the recipes to. The 0.11.0 change
(`docs/plan-cut-end-spikes.md`: a `cut` end pins the edge samples its corner moved, bounded by how far the corner moved)
can redraw a glyph whose `cut` end moves its corner past edge samples, in the recipes that draw a `cut` end. Two of the ten recipes do:
`grot` and `geometric`. Their outline digests moved and the other eight did not, and the rule in `genres.md` section 6 is that a
stale digest means a new reading and a new record, not an edited digest. This record says what was read on 2026-11-25,
what was concluded per recipe, and the digests of the outlines as drawn now.

## What was reviewed

- The page built by `node scripts/concepts/build-page.js --template round1 --concepts templates/concepts.genres.js --content scripts/concepts/content.genres.json`
  with `scripts/gen.js` as 0.11.0 draws it, served with the built neutral Sampler beside it, and shot by `scripts/shoot.py`
  at 1280 px, 2x, `--height 16000 --dump-dom --family Sampler --font-url /fonts/Sampler-VF.woff2`, in headless Chrome 158.
  The DOM reported all ten recipes drawn (`data-fd-rendered="3330"`, probe `bad 0`). The screenshots are not kept in the repository.
- Read by one model reviewer (the implementer, not a fresh reviewer), as in the 2026-11-22 re-read: a targeted reading of
  `grot` and `geometric` only, the two recipes with a `cut` end. The other eight recipes set no `cut` end, and their digests equal
  the 2026-11-22 ones under every node major, so nothing was re-judged for them and their verdicts carry over with their old date.
- What was read: the specimen line of each recipe (the letters, digits and punctuation the page sets at about 40 px) at Regular,
  weight 400, from the 2x crop enlarged twice; and each recipe's headline and chapter headings at the heavier weights the page
  sets, 500 and 600. The measured set of letters with a moved `cut` end is `C G J S a c e f g r s t`, `ff fi`, `1 2 3 5 6 9` and
  `$ & ' * , ; ?`; the page's specimen line sets every letter of that set except `ff`, `fi`, `$`, `*` and `;` (the headline sets `ff` and `fi`),
  and no accented form. A second reading (also 2026-11-25, same reviewer) covered those keys. It used a page built from a scratch copy of
  `content.genres.json` (not in the repository) whose lineup text was `ff fi $ * ; š ā é á à è â ê ä ë ç Ç ©`, shot at 1280 px, 2x, and read
  the `grot` and `geometric` rows from 2x crops enlarged twice, at Regular (mult 1) and at Bold (mult 1.5, from a scratch copy of the page with
  the weight select preset to Bold, since the page has no query parameter for the weight). Keys read that way: `ff fi $ * ;` and `š ā é á à è â ê ä ë ç Ç ©`.
  Medium (mult 1.25) was not read in that pass. The first reading's other keys were read at Regular in the specimen line, and at the heavier weights only in the headline words.
- What was seen, per recipe: no thin spike at any cut corner, in either recipe, at either weight. A step remains where a curved
  terminal meets its cut corner, at the foot of `t`, the top of `f` and the tail of `J` in both recipes, and at the tail of `g` and `y` in the headline of `grot`.
  It is the step the 0.11.0 plan names (`references/generator.md` section 5): the cut moves the corner along the end tangent, so the
  corner stands off the curved edge. The implementer read it as a small tooth at 44 px and up, not as a spike. The operator, reading the page
  on 2026-11-25, saw it as a thorn on `t` and `r` in both recipes; outline renders of 0.11.0 at mult 1 and 1.5 show a sharp upward tick on
  the inside of the `t` foot and under the `r` terminal. It is recorded in `genres.md` section 7 and tracked as issue #27.
- What the second reading saw: no thin spike at a cut corner in either recipe, at Regular or at Bold, on any of those keys. At Regular the
  top terminal of the `f` of `ff` and `fi` carries a small tapering flick in both recipes; at Bold the flick is larger and reads as a hooked
  flag of the `f` terminal, not as a detached spike. At Bold the accents of `á à è â ê ä ë š` sit hard against the letter and fuse with it, and
  the cedilla of `ç Ç` is a heavy blob; those are accent placement and weight collisions, not cut corners. The step (thorn) where a curved
  terminal meets its cut corner shows on `c e ç Ç` and on the `a` family at Bold, as it did on `t f J`. None of this changes a verdict word.
- Limits of the reading: one reviewer, at specimen size with two enlargements. Not every glyph was read at Regular and at Bold: the keys
  `C G J S c g r s t`, `1 2 3 5 6 9`, `& , '` and `?` were read at Regular in the specimen line and at the heavier weights in the headline words only.
  `ff fi $ * ;` and the accented forms were read at Regular and Bold, not at Medium. No ligature other than `ff` and `fi`, and no italic (the page sets none).
  The reviewer was the implementer, not a fresh reviewer.
- The reading changed no verdict word. `grot` stays **partly** and `geometric` stays **no**; both take the date of this reading
  and a sentence about the cut terminals.
- Operator ruling, 2026-11-25: the operator read the page and agreed with both verdict words, `grot` **partly** and `geometric` **no**.

## Verdicts

| recipe | verdict | date | what was seen |
|---|---|---|---|
| didone | partly | 2026-11-22 | Moderate contrast because of the hairline floor, and equal-weight diagonals on A V W M N v w x y, so it reads as a light modern, not a Didone. The diagonals need an expansion model, which is out of scope |
| fatface | no | 2026-11-22 | Stems are about 1/6 of the cap height even at the Bold; W past its range cannot reach 1/3-cap stems without collapsing the counters. It needs new shapes |
| scotch | reaches | 2026-11-22 | A near-twin of the Didone at these sizes; the bracket and the a terminal are what differ. Also shipped and reviewed as Bract's foil on hopsafari.com (2026-11-15). The italic is refused (section 4) |
| clarendon | no | 2026-11-22 | The serifs are short pointed wedges, not square bracketed slabs, so it reads as a wedge serif. A longer serifLen with serifGrow 0 might fix it; untested. Rendering defects: section 7 |
| slab | reaches | 2026-11-22 | Square unbracketed slabs at stem weight, low contrast, in Rockwell or Memphis proportion. Rendering defects: section 7 |
| grot | partly | 2026-11-25 | A monoline neo-grotesque with level-cut joints and a straight-leg R; the double-storey g with its sharp ear and the hooked a read humanist, which needs skeleton work. Seen after the cut default was fixed (section 7). The cut terminals were re-read on 2026-11-25 after the 0.11.0 clip: no spike, and a thorn where a curved terminal meets its cut corner on t, r, f and J, visible at Regular (issue #27) (section 7) |
| geometric | no | 2026-11-25 | A wide humanist sans, not Futura-like: it needs a circular O, a single-storey a and pointed apexes, all unbuilt. The cut terminals were re-read on 2026-11-25 after the 0.11.0 clip: no spike, and a thorn where a curved terminal meets its cut corner on t, r and f, visible at Regular (issue #27) (section 7) |
| reverse | partly | 2026-11-22 | Reverse contrast, but light, at typewriter weight; a larger W and a lower thin might reach a French Clarendon (untested). Rendering defects, and a lump where the bowls of P, B and R meet the thin stem: section 7 |
| opsz-text | reaches | 2026-11-22 | As a pair: a wider, looser text cut with a taller x-height beside a narrower, higher-contrast display cut. The display cut is close to the Didone |
| opsz-display | reaches | 2026-11-22 | As a pair: a wider, looser text cut with a taller x-height beside a narrower, higher-contrast display cut. The display cut is close to the Didone |

## Outline digests

For each recipe: sha256 over `[mult, key, polygons]` for every key of `G` in sorted order, roman, mult 1, 1.25 then 1.5, each coordinate
rounded to 0.01, as `tests/test_genres.py::current_digests()` computes it, from `scripts/gen.js` as 0.11.0 draws. Eight digests equal the
2026-11-22 record's (`didone`, `fatface`, `scotch`, `clarendon`, `slab`, `reverse`, `opsz-text`, `opsz-display`) and two differ (`grot`, `geometric`).
The node 26 digests come from `current_digests()` under node 26.13.1. The node 20 and 22 digests come from the same `DIGEST` script and the
same `GEN` and `GENRES_JS` arguments, run on this machine, not on CI, under node 20.20.2 and node 22.23.5 (the versions the 2026-11-22
record's CI run used), each fetched with `npx --yes -p node@<version> node -e "$DIGEST" <gen.js> <concepts.genres.js>` and run twice with
byte-equal output. All three majors agree on every digest. The first CI run of `tests/test_genres.py` on the branch (`release-acceptance`) is the
check on a CI machine. The test recomputes the digests under the running node and fails with "the genre verdict for <id> is stale" when the digest recorded
for that node major differs, and with instructions to record it when the major is not listed. If it fails after a deliberate change to a
recipe or to `gen.js`, the remedy is a new render review (`genres.md` section 6) and a new record, not editing the digest.
